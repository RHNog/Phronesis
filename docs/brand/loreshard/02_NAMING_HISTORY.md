# Naming History

> **INTERNAL — CONFIDENTIAL — PRE-LAUNCH BRAND STRATEGY**
> **PRELIMINARY LEGAL / BRAND CLEARANCE — NOT LEGAL ADVICE**
> **HUMAN / COUNSEL VERIFICATION REQUIRED**

This document preserves how the search moved from the Phronesis internal codename to the LoreShard working finalist. Search findings below are founder-supplied historical desk-research conclusions. They have not been converted into legal opinions by this archive.

## Search Evolution

### 1. Literal collectible intelligence: Curio

`CurioLogic` initially expressed the business well: unusual or collectible objects interpreted through logic and decision intelligence. The direction weakened when prior research surfaced an active `CURIO` antique/collectible AI identification, valuation, and collection-management product. The adjacency created a meaningful related-company and extension-perception risk.

**Lesson:** an excellent semantic fit is insufficient when the root already occupies the same user problem.

### 2. Guidance and orientation: Beacon, Sentinel, Compass

Beacon, Sentinel, Compass, Bearing, and Waypoint captured guidance, monitoring, and direction. They were crowded across software, AI, cybersecurity, logistics, analytics, and enterprise tooling. Sentinel also pulled the product toward security rather than commerce intelligence.

**Lesson:** broad guidance metaphors are credible but rarely distinctive enough for a scalable software master brand.

### 3. Greek practical-intelligence language

Explored terms included `Skopos`, `Pharos`, `Metron`, `Noesis`, `Praxis`, `Kairos`, `Telos`, `Logos`, `Sema`, `Axios`, `Arche`, `Kanon`, and `Nous`.

- **Skopos** had excellent aim, purpose, target, and observer meaning but prior research found occupied domains and active software, finance, research, and application uses.
- **Pharos** carried the lighthouse/beacon metaphor but appeared crowded among enterprise, IT, and software marks.
- The wider family carried intellectual weight but often recreated the pronunciation, explanation, or crowding problems of Phronesis.

**Lesson:** classical-language depth alone did not solve memorability, availability, or commercial immediacy.

### 4. Latin discernment and measurement

Explored terms included `Cerno`, `Fides`, `Scio`, `Signum`, `Lumen`, `Norma`, `Custos`, `Ratio`, `Arbiter`, and `Specula`.

- **Cerno** meant discern, perceive, and decide, but prior research found adjacent AI, decision-management, and business-intelligence use.
- **Arbiter** expressed judgment but was crowded in decision, AI, and infrastructure contexts.
- **Specula** offered a watchtower/lookout meaning but had weak fit and possible unwanted associations.

**Lesson:** compact classical words were usually already active, difficult to own, or tonally misaligned.

### 5. Scientific and market-intelligence abstraction

Ideas included `Datum`, `Parallax`, `Aperture`, `Vantage`, `Vector`, `Signal`, `Caliber`, `Metric`, `Prism`, `Spectrum`, `Contour`, `Axiom`, `Scalar`, `Gradient`, `Vertex`, `Zenith`, `Azimuth`, `Locus`, `Index`, `Trace`, and `Resolve`.

These names provided technical credibility but often felt like generic or invented SaaS labels without enough emotional or collectible-cultural weight.

**Lessons:**

- semantic and emotional resonance matter alongside analytical credibility;
- domain/namespace screening should occur before emotional commitment;
- the name should support a story that users can understand without sounding manufactured.

### 6. Lira family

- **LiraSight** conveyed elegant vision and foresight, but “Sight” could become “Site” in Brazilian speech. It may work better as a module than a master brand.
- **LiraSense** was rejected after prior research surfaced `LyraSense`/similar AI-software collision risk.
- **LiraWise** was the strongest Lira option and received a historical **GO WITH CONDITIONS** result. It remained abstract, sat among crowded LIRA/LYRA AI roots, carried a `Wise` fintech/currency shadow, and did not create founder-level emotional conviction.

**Result:** `LiraWise` remains fallback only; `LiraSight` remains a possible module name subject to future clearance.

### 7. Voyage and celestial lane

`ArgoWise` combined voyage/mission/valuable-prize meaning with practical intelligence. It weakened because:

- `ARGOWISE` is visually contained within `CARGOWISE`;
- Argo is crowded in software, Kubernetes, Argo CD, and Argo Workflows;
- Fiat Argo creates Brazilian market noise.

The founder explicitly instructed that `ArgoWise` not be suggested again unless requested. `OrionProof` did not survive as a master brand.

### 8. Metis and Meti lane

The Metis concept strongly matched practical intelligence and cunning wisdom. Explored names included `MetisWise`, `MetisValue`, `MetisProof`, `CardMetis`, `MetiLens`, `MetiPilot`, and `MetiValue`.

The entire family was rejected because prior research surfaced active exact or adjacent METIS software/AI use, Canadian Métis identity sensitivity, and a founder-identified vulgarity risk for `Meti` constructions in Portuguese.

**Permanent rule:** avoid all `Meti_____` constructions.

### 9. Pallas family

Pallas/Athena conveyed strategic wisdom, disciplined intelligence, craft, defense, and counsel. Candidates included `PallasMind`, `PallasPraxis`, `PallasMetron`, `PallasAegis`, `PallasKey`, `PallasLens`, `PallasAxis`, `PallasLogic`, `PallasCore`, `PallasLedger`, `PallasMetric`, `PallasScan`, and `PallasPrice`.

The family initially looked promising, but later cross-evaluation found substantial Pallas use in AI, automation, software consulting, information technology, machine vision, and visual inspection. `PallasMind` survived as a possible hold; the family is paused unless LoreShard fails.

### 10. Fantasy artifact without direct fantasy IP

The founder responded to the idea of an artifact that reveals distant knowledge, similar in archetype to a seeing stone. Direct Tolkien names were ruled out, including Palantir, Silmaril, Arkenstone, Mithril, Numenor, Gondor, Valinor, Eldar, Aulë, Durin, Orthanc, and Istari. These are protected/licensed properties, and Palantir is also a major data/software company.

The safe lane became archetypal vocabulary: lore, rune, shard, orb, glass, stone, forge, lens, ledger, vault, codex, and relic.

`RuneLens` resonated but was rejected after prior research found active exact/near-exact software use for OCR, analysis, and game decision support.

**Lesson:** borrow the archetype of revealed knowledge, never another universe’s protected proper nouns.

### 11. MarketShard and the lasting thesis

`MarketShard` created the strongest strategic concept in the search:

- the market is fragmented;
- every provider is a partial view;
- evidence must be assembled;
- **no single price is the market**.

The name itself was not adopted because `MarketSharp` is an active SaaS/CRM/business-management platform and the names differ by one final consonant. Spelling, phonetic, SEO, and mistaken-association risk were high. `SHARD` also carried database, crypto, and software crowding.

**Result:** the MarketShard name was rejected, but its central proposition survives: **No single price is the market.**

### 12. Lore family and final survivor

Lore brought accumulated knowledge, provenance, market memory, and collectible-cultural resonance. The search eliminated or deprioritized:

- `MarketLore` — taken or premium in prior checking.
- `TradeLore` — taken/active.
- `LoreLens` — active traces.
- `LoreLogic` — product/trademark footprints were not clean.
- `TrueLore` — provenance/authentication-adjacent concern.
- `DeepLore` — AI/company collision.
- `LoreOS`, `LoreGuild`, `LoreAtlas` — active products or apps.
- `LoreForge`, `LoreKeeper`, `LoreCraft` — worldbuilding/TTRPG/Warcraft crowding.
- `LoreHouse` — existing uses and weak leadership.
- `LoreMind`, `LoreWise`, `LoreSense` — generic AI/financial/sense-family problems.
- `LoreX` — Lorex security-camera confusion.
- `LoreMark` — “Lorem”/placeholder reading.
- `LoreEdge` — spelling trap.
- `LoreSage` — Sage software/commerce shadow.
- `LoreStand` — booth/kiosk meaning, especially Brazilian `estande` association.

`LoreShard` survived because the joined compound appeared comparatively sparse in preliminary research, the founder acquired the `.com` and `.com.br`, the name preserved the MarketShard thesis without the MarketSharp problem, and the accumulated-knowledge-plus-fragment metaphor fit collectible-commerce intelligence.
